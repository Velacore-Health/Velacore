# How Dourado judges design

Draft v1, Nov 6 2026. Written by Kevin at Dourado's request and sent to the Velacore project the same day. Dourado reads this before it counts as law. When he corrects something, edit this file the same day and bump the version line.

## How to use this file
Read it at the start of any design or page task, together with `docs/BRAND.md`. Load the craft skills first: frontend-design, emil-design-eng, web-design-guidelines, mobile-native, break-ui. This file says what they cannot: what this particular man notices.

## How he works with you
- He is not technical. Plain words. When he has to act on his own screen, one step per message, exactly what to click and what he should see, wait for his answer.
- No em dashes, no parentheses, no emojis in anything he will publish.
- He grades bluntly and wants to be graded bluntly. A number out of ten is welcome.
- He should not be the one who finds your mistakes. If he catches something you could have checked, the fix is a new check, not an apology.
- Nothing goes live, gets posted, pushed or deployed until he says so. Show first.

## What he notices
1. Alignment and room. Logos of different widths in a column too narrow made names zigzag. Give every slot room for its widest item and check it.
2. Never clipped words. A product name cut to two lines read as broken. Every label must fit its longest real text. Check at 375 wide that nothing overflows or sits past the edge.
3. The phone comes first. Design at 375 to 390, then widen.
4. Facts, not just links. A card listed two formats while four were live. A review includes facts: every edition, option, price status and claim matches what is live. Write a script and run it before showing him and before every deploy.
5. No stale facts. A price drifted from the real store and every price came off. If a number belongs to another system, link to it, do not copy it.
6. Not templated. Cards with uppercase sans on a warm color, pill stickers and generated photos were called AI slop. Everything needs something only this brand could say or show. Fewer words, more white space, one accent, a note that sounds like a person.
7. Quiet typography. No small label above every heading, one-color headlines, no middle dot strings, no trailing arrows on buttons.
8. Tactile, not flat. A lit button and a low film grain were approved. When you add a global texture, check boxes that paint their own background, they can show flat grey.
9. The logo is never redrawn. Use the supplied asset. If missing, ask.
10. The brand guide wins. If it is wrong, tell him and edit it.
11. Reference first. When he sends a link or video as a style reference, break down what fits and clashes. Skip hype, income claims and fear.
12. Motion is rare. One deliberate moment beats effects everywhere. Respect reduced motion.

## The review pass before showing him anything
1. Every internal link, image and file exists. Outside links fetched once.
2. Facts checked against the live source, by script.
3. Overflow scan at 375 and 1280: page wider than the screen, elements past the edge, clipped text, images that failed to load.
4. Look at the key pages at both widths with your own eyes.
5. Compare against the brand guide, then this file.
6. Then show him, with a before and after if it replaces something. Report in two or three lines.

## Health brand note
Velacore is a health brand. Design review does not replace compliance review. Claims, wording near products, disclosures and anything that reads as medical advice follow the compliance documents already built. If a design choice makes a claim bigger, such as a headline that promises a result, stop and ask.

## Finding the gaps
Build one real page. Kevin reviews it against this file and says what misses. Every miss becomes a new line or a new check. Kevin's own project rules block him from reading the Velacore folder, so that loop runs only when Dourado says so and opens the one page to him.
