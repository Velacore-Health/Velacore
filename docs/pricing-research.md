# Velacore pricing research — Nov 2026

Goal: pick price points that win volume without the business breaking when patients churn.
Sources are mostly 2026 comparison/affiliate sites and vendor blogs (listed at the end); treat all
figures as directional and verify on provider sites before quoting them anywhere.

## 1. What the market charges (month-to-month, compounded)

| Product | Low end (usually with a catch) | Mainstream cluster | Median | Velacore today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide inj. | $139–178 all-in (Mochi), $99 teaser months | $149–249 | ~$199–249 | $299 |
| Tirzepatide inj. | $119–149 (annual prepay to hit $86) | $179–299 | $249 (93 programs) / $329 (28 providers) | $349 |
| NAD+ inj. | $79 first month, $90/mo on 6-mo prepay | $129–224 | ~$150–180 | $279 / 70 days |
| Sermorelin | $49 first month, $99/mo (Embody) | $150–250 | ~$180–200 | $329 / 56 days |
| Glutathione | $57/mo (12-mo prepay) | $79–149 | ~$120 | $199 (not on OpenLoop) |

Patterns that matter:
- **The cheapest headline prices are almost never month-to-month.** They are annual prepay, a
  12-week pack "charged as" a lump sum, or a first-month teaser that jumps 2x in month two.
- **Mainstream buyers see $149–249 for semaglutide and $179–299 for tirzepatide.** Velacore's
  current $299/$349 sits at the top of both ranges.
- **First-month intros of $99–149 are standard** and expected by shoppers.
- **Membership + medication** (Mochi: $79 + $99) is a common structure; it reads cheaper than
  it is. We don't need to copy it.

## 2. Retention is brutal — this changes the math more than price does

- ~19% of obesity GLP-1 patients fill only one prescription (IQVIA).
- ~32% discontinue within 180 days; by 1 year only ~10% remain persistent; ~85% of non-diabetic
  patients stop within 2 years.
- Why they quit: side effects (~27%), cost (~14%), non-adherence (~11%).

Takeaways:
- A lower price helps retention, but only on the ~14% who leave over cost. Side effects are the
  bigger leak, and the fix there is onboarding and support (OpenLoop's 24/7 support, dose
  titration, nausea management), not price.
- **Plan on an average tenure of ~3–4 months for cash-pay GLP-1 patients.** Anything better is
  upside. Every price decision below assumes 3.5 months.
- **Prepaid 12-week plans are the single best retention tool**: they turn month-two churn into a
  guaranteed three months. OpenLoop's cut is about the same per month on 12-week plans as on
  monthly, so prepaid plans cost us almost no margin.

## 3. What it costs to get a patient

- Blended cost to acquire a *paying* GLP-1 patient: roughly **$150–250** (compounded, fully
  loaded $180–320). Meta is the volume engine; one clinic case reported $145 per enrolled patient.
- Lead cost $30–75, then intake/approval drop-off.
- Lower price raises conversion, which lowers acquisition cost. That is the real engine of a
  volume strategy, and it has to be measured, not assumed.

## 5. Where to land

Position: **below the median, above the floor, with a strong first-month offer and a 12-week
plan as the default.** Volume comes from the intro price and the prepaid plan, not from racing
to $99.

| Product | First month (new patients) | Month-to-month | 12-week plan (default) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide inj. | **$149** | **$229** | **$199/mo** ($597 up front) |
| Tirzepatide inj. | **$199** | **$279** | **$249/mo** ($747 up front) |
| Microdosing GLP-1 | $129 | **$179** | $159/mo |
| NAD+ inj. | $129 | **$199** | $169/mo |
| Sermorelin | $129 | **$199** | $169/mo |

Why this shape:
- The **microdosing GLP-1 program is the built-in volume tier.** OpenLoop keeps only ~$89 on it,
  the patient price of $179 is well under the market, and it fits maintenance and price-sensitive
  buyers. Promote it as the entry point instead of discounting the full-dose program to the floor.
- $229/$279 month-to-month reads as a real discount against the $299/$349 you show today and
  against the median, while keeping LTV ÷ CAC above 2.3 even on pessimistic assumptions.
- The 12-week price undercuts the month-to-month price by $30, which is the lever that moves
  people into the prepaid plan.
- $149/$199 first month matches what shoppers expect from the category and is cheap to fund:
  the first-month discount on 10 patients costs under $1,000 of margin, less than one month of
  OpenLoop's $3,000 fee.

**Then A/B test.** Launchpad's funnel-split tool lets you run $199 vs $229 for semaglutide on the
same ads. If $199 lifts conversion enough to bring CAC under ~$120, move down. If it doesn't,
hold $229. Decide from the data within the first 60 days.

## 6. Guardrails

- Keep the intro and the standard price on the same page ("$149 first month, then $229/mo,
  cancel anytime"). Hidden price jumps cause ad rejections and refund disputes.
- Never advertise a "$99/mo" that needs an annual prepay to be true.
- No equivalence claims to Ozempic/Mounjaro, no before/after promises; include "compounded
  medications are not FDA-approved" and "prescription required."
- Cheaper price does not fix side-effect churn. Pair pricing with a first-two-weeks check-in and
  nausea support, which is where most of the early drop-off comes from.
- Revisit prices when OpenLoop's cost sheet changes (it is marked "subject to change").
