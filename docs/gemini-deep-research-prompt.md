# Gemini Deep Research prompt for the Velacore product look

I run a telehealth brand called Velacore Health. Independent licensed clinicians prescribe and 503A compounding pharmacies fill compounded prescription medications: tirzepatide, semaglutide, a low-dose GLP-1 program, NAD+ and sermorelin. I am designing the product images for my website: vials, dropper bottles and nasal sprays with a Velacore Health label, photographed in a clean studio style. The first images I generated look generic. I need them to look official, premium and trustworthy to a first-time customer, the way a real pharmacy product does.

Research and report on these questions, with sources:

1. What a real compounded-prescription vial label looks like in the United States. What text elements pharmacy labels usually carry, how they are laid out, what type sizes and weights, where the Rx only statement sits, how lot, strength and beyond-use lines are shown. Cite USP, FDA and state board guidance where it exists. I want to know what a mock label should and should not show so it reads as authentic without pretending to be a dispensed prescription.

2. What makes pharmaceutical packaging read as premium and credible. Study how high-end pharmacy and clinical skincare brands design labels and vials: paper stock, matte versus gloss, white space, type pairing, one accent color, printed cap seals, crimp finishes, tamper bands. Give concrete design rules I can hand to a designer.

3. How leading direct-to-consumer telehealth brands photograph their compounded products on their websites. Describe the lighting, backdrops, camera angle, props or lack of props, how the vial sits, how they show kits and pens, and what they avoid. Compare at least five brands and say which look most trustworthy and why.

4. What visual mistakes make product images look fake or AI generated: misspelled label text, impossible glass, floating shadows, wrong vial proportions, color casts, glow effects. Give me a checklist to reject bad images.

5. The real physical specs. Standard sizes and proportions of a 2 ml, 5 ml and 10 ml pharmaceutical glass vial, the aluminum crimp and the plastic flip-off cap, amber glass dropper bottles, and nasal spray bottles, so a generated image has correct proportions.

Deliver: a one-page label specification with exact text lines and layout for a mock Velacore Health label; a one-page photography specification for a consistent product set, including lens, light, backdrop color, angle and framing; the rejection checklist; and a list of ten reference images with links that show the look I should aim for. Do not include any health or efficacy claims about the medications. Keep it to what a designer and an image generator need.
