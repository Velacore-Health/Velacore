## What we will not write, even though people search it

- Outcome and benefit pages: weight loss results, before and after, benefits, for inflammation, for menopause, for PCOS, anti aging. These are claims.
- Dose charts and calculators. Dosing is the clinician's.
- Brand-name comparisons. Phrases like compounded tirzepatide vs a brand name are heavily searched, but comparing a compounded drug to an FDA-approved brand is the riskiest topic for LegitScript and the FDA. Held until the attorney or LegitScript guidance clears a wording.
- Competitor names, Reddit bait, cheapest claims.

## Site structure

```
Home
  Program pages, 6      one keyword family each, already live
  How it works          to build, process searches
  Pricing               to build, cost searches, one table from the program data
  Clinicians            to build, trust
  Learn                 hub, lists every article by topic
    learn/<article>     one question per article, links to its program page
  FAQ
```

Every article: one question in the title, a direct answer in the first paragraph, plain sections, sources from the FDA, NIH or other primary bodies, a short question list, a link to the matching program, and Find your program at the end. Each carries the date it was reviewed.

## Trust signals Google wants on health sites

- A named medical reviewer on each article. Open: ask OpenLoop whether a clinician can be credited.
- Sources linked on every article.
- A real About page with the company name, address and contact.
- Reviewed dates kept current.

## Rhythm
