# Design audit and competitor research, Nov 11 2026

Asked by Dourado after the hero video-call image failed: review ARSYNL Rx again, audit our design, and find other sites to study. Every site below was opened in the browser at 1280 px wide on Nov 11 2026. Their claims are recorded so we know what not to copy; none of it is a source for our own copy.

## What the strong sites do above the fold

| Site | Hero image | What else is in the first screen | The hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARSYNL Rx, arsynl.com | A four-photo collage of people: gym, desk, couple, outdoors | A scrolling credentials ticker above, three goal chips, one button, a four-badge trust bar right under the hero | Trust and the founder's veteran story |
| Eden, tryeden.com | No single photo. Big colored program cards, each with a branded vial: GLP-1, Sermorelin, NAD+ | Member count, free shipping, FSA and HSA, a promo bar | The products themselves, one tap from the first screen |
| Mochi Health, joinmochi.com | Product shot: pens and a vial floating on soft blue | Two price cards in the hero, a first-month offer bar on top | Price, shown before anything else |
| Henry, henrymeds.com | A woman laughing at her laptop, with a separate photo of a clinician set in the corner like a video call | Program chips with small faces right under the hero | Telehealth made obvious, built from two photos by design, not one AI image |
| Ro, ro.co/weight-loss | A photo strip: people, pens, a pill | Two buttons, three short bullets beside the headline | Eligibility and insurance |
| Hims, hims.com/weight-loss | Editorial: warm gradient, a large pill, portraits | One button | Brand mood |

Patterns that repeat on almost every site:
1. The first screen shows either the product or the price, usually both.
2. A short row of trust facts sits right next to or under the headline.
3. The visitor can pick a program from the first screen.
4. Images show the product or a recognizable telehealth moment, never an unrelated daily-life scene.

## Our home page against that

| Item | Velacore today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Hero image | A woman pouring water in her kitchen | Says nothing about prescriptions, clinicians or the product. Dourado's read was right |
| Product in the first screen | None; the vials start one scroll down | Every product-led competitor shows it at once |
| Price in the first screen | None; prices are on the cards below | Mochi and ARSYNL put a price up front. Ours is competitive: from $129 for the first month |
| Trust facts | A moving ticker, hidden from screen readers | The facts are right but move and are easy to miss. A still row of four reads faster |
| Program choice | Second section | Eden and Henry let the visitor pick a program in the first screen |
| Lede | Four sentences | Competitors use one or two. vc-copy follow-up already open |
| Claims | Clean | Several competitors make claims we never will, listed below |

## Recommendation for the hero

Product-led, built from images we already have, no new Gemini image needed:

1. Replace the kitchen photo with the tirzepatide splash vial. It is 3 by 4, the exact shape of the current hero frame. The illustrative-image line goes under it.
2. Add one price line under the headline, pulled from the program data: from $129 your first month, shipping included.
3. Swap the moving ticker for a still row of four facts under the buttons: licensed clinicians in all 50 states, 503A compounding pharmacies, shipping included, refunded in full if not prescribed.
4. Shorten the lede to one or two sentences through vc-copy.

Second choice, if Dourado wants people up top: the Henry pattern, a real-looking home photo with a separate clinician card in the corner. Our clinician image must not show a face, so the corner card would be the hands-and-stethoscope photo from step 2, cropped square.

Everything goes through vc-reviewer before a push.

## Do not copy

- Outcome and efficacy claims: "Weight loss that works", "Lose 20% body weight on average", "Less fat, more lean mass", "Feel like yourself again", "proven therapies", invented statistics like "50% of adults show low hormone markers".
- Brand-name drug references and FDA badges next to compounded products.
- Them-versus-others comparison tables, member counts we cannot prove, and testimonials until we have real consented ones.
- Products outside our approved five.
