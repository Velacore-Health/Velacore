How Velacore works

Velacore coordinates. An independent licensed clinician decides. A licensed 503A compounding pharmacy fills. This page sets out each step in order. Prescription required.

The steps at a glance

  1. Online intake. You pick a program and answer questions about your health, history and medications.
  2. Clinician review. A physician or nurse practitioner licensed in your state reviews your intake and decides whether to prescribe.
  3. Pharmacy fulfillment. If you are prescribed, a licensed 503A compounding pharmacy prepares your medication and ships it to you.
  4. Ongoing reviews and support. The clinician reviews your intake updates on a set schedule, and patient support is available 24 hours a day, every day.

Every program is prescription only. Starting a visit is not a guarantee of treatment.

Online intake

You start by choosing a program. If you are not sure which one, Find your program walks you through the options. The clinician still makes the final decision on which program, if any, is appropriate.

The intake is a set of questions about your goals, your health history and the medications you take. It runs on our telehealth services partner's secure pages, not on this website, so you will see their name there instead of ours.

The Semaglutide and Tirzepatide programs are for adults 18 to 74 with a body mass index of 27 or above, or 25 or above with a related health condition. The Microdosing GLP-1 program is for adults 18 to 74 with a body mass index over 20. Each program page states who the program is for.

Clinician review

A physician or nurse practitioner licensed in your state reviews your intake. The clinician decides whether to prescribe, based solely on clinical judgment. Velacore has no say in that decision.

Most reviews happen without a live appointment. A video visit is required for the first prescription in Arkansas, the District of Columbia, Delaware, Kansas, Mississippi, New Mexico, Rhode Island and West Virginia. Everywhere else a video visit is optional, and you can request one at any time at no extra cost.

Clinician hours are 7am to 7pm Central Time. More about who the clinicians are and how they are credentialed is on the clinicians page.

Pharmacy fulfillment

If the clinician prescribes, a licensed 503A compounding pharmacy prepares your medication to that prescription and ships it to you. Ongoing plans ship every four weeks.

Compounded medications are not FDA approved and are not reviewed by the FDA for safety or efficacy.

For the Semaglutide and Tirzepatide programs, the clinician also orders baseline labs at a national laboratory at no cost to you. In most cases you can start medication before the results are back. For other programs the clinician will tell you if labs are recommended.

A 503A pharmacy prepares medication for one named patient from a valid prescription. Our article on what a 503A compounding pharmacy is explains the term.

Ongoing reviews and support

A prescription is not a one-time event. The clinician reviews your intake updates on a schedule that depends on the program.

  • Semaglutide and Tirzepatide. Each month you update a short intake. The clinician reviews it with your labs and decides whether to hold or change the dose. Once your dose is steady, reviews move to every three months.
  • Microdosing GLP-1. The first prescription is a 4-week supply. After that you update a short intake every 12 weeks and the clinician reviews it.
  • NAD+, Glutathione and Sermorelin. The first prescription is a 4-week supply. The clinician reviews an intake update at 3 to 4 weeks before writing a 12-week refill, and reviews continue every 12 weeks.

Nothing about your prescription changes without a clinician's review.

Patient support is available 24 hours a day, every day. You can message your clinician or request a video visit through your patient portal. If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

If the clinician does not prescribe

A prescription is not guaranteed. Prescriptions are issued only when the clinician decides one is clinically appropriate.

If the clinician does not prescribe, nothing is ordered and your program payment is refunded in full to your original payment method.

After you start, you can cancel at any time for any reason. You can request a refund for any month whose medication has not yet been ordered. Medications are non-returnable once dispensed, as required by federal and state pharmacy law. The full terms are in the refund policy.

What Velacore does and does not do

Velacore Health is a telehealth coordination platform. Here is what that covers.

  • We run this website.
  • We explain the programs and show their prices.
  • We point you to the secure intake.
  • We answer questions about how the service works.

Here is what it does not cover.

  • We do not practice medicine or give medical advice.
  • We do not prescribe, and we have no say in whether a clinician prescribes.
  • We do not make, compound or ship medication.
  • We do not hold your card details. Our telehealth services partner bills you.
  • We do not promise that you will be prescribed anything.

The intake, the clinician review, billing and patient support are run by our telehealth services partner on its own secure pages.

Common questions

Do I have to speak with a clinician live?

In most states, no. Most reviews happen without a live appointment. A video visit is required for the first prescription in Arkansas, the District of Columbia, Delaware, Kansas, Mississippi, New Mexico, Rhode Island and West Virginia.

Can I start medication before my lab results are back?

For the Semaglutide and Tirzepatide programs, in most cases yes. The clinician reviews the results at your first monthly review. That decision belongs to the clinician.

How often does medication ship?

Ongoing plans ship every four weeks.

Does Velacore decide whether I am prescribed?

No. The independent licensed clinician decides, based solely on clinical judgment. Velacore coordinates access and education.
