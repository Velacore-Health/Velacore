Peptides, explained simply

No jargon. Just the basics, so you can make an informed decision with a clinician.

What are peptides?

Your body is built from proteins. Picture them as long strings of beads, where each bead is an amino acid. A peptide is simply a short string of those same beads.

Because they are small, peptides act like the body's messengers, little signals that tell cells what to do. Your body already makes thousands of them naturally, every day. In a medical setting, a clinician may prescribe a specific peptide as part of a treatment plan.

Are they safe?

Safety depends on the person, the peptide and the dose, which is exactly why every program here is prescription only. A licensed clinician reviews your health history and decides whether a peptide is appropriate for you, and it is compounded by a licensed 503A pharmacy. Like any medication, peptides can have side effects and are not right for everyone. That is a conversation to have with your clinician.

What if I stop taking them?

Everyone responds differently, and it depends on the treatment and your daily habits. Some changes people work toward are supported by ongoing routines, like sleep, nutrition and movement, while other effects may gradually fade after stopping. There is no one-size-fits-all answer, so whether to continue, adjust or stop is a decision to make with your clinician.

How do I know if they are right for me?

The only way to know is a clinical evaluation. Start an online visit. A licensed clinician reviews your goals and health history and tells you whether treatment is appropriate. If it is not, they will tell you that too, and your payment comes back.

How is it taken?

It depends on what is prescribed. Many peptide and GLP-1 treatments are given as a small subcutaneous self-injection, a tiny needle just under the skin, usually weekly or daily. Some programs come as a tablet, a dropper or a nasal spray. Your prescription ships with step-by-step instructions, and support can walk you through your first dose.

How are they made?

Prescribed treatments are prepared, or compounded, by licensed 503A compounding pharmacies in the U.S. that follow strict quality, sterility and testing standards. Compounding lets a pharmacy prepare a medication to a clinician's specific prescription. Compounded medications are not FDA approved.

Peptides vs. GLP-1s: what is the difference?

GLP-1s like semaglutide and tirzepatide are actually a type of peptide. "Peptide" is the broad category, short chains of amino acids that signal the body, and GLP-1 receptor agonists are one specific, well-known group within it. Whether any of them is appropriate for you is a clinical decision made by the clinician.

This section is general education, not medical advice. Whether any peptide is appropriate or safe for you is determined solely by a licensed clinician.

Questions, answered

What is Velacore Health?

Velacore Health is a telehealth platform. We coordinate access to licensed clinicians and 503A compounding pharmacies. We do not provide, guarantee, or facilitate any medical services, prescriptions, or treatments. All prescribing decisions are made solely by the independent clinician.

Do I need a prescription?

Yes. Every program is prescription only. You complete an online intake, and a licensed clinician decides whether to prescribe based solely on clinical judgment. Prescriptions are issued only when clinically appropriate.

Does Velacore prescribe or provide medical care?

No. Velacore coordinates access and education. Licensed clinicians make all clinical decisions, and licensed 503A pharmacies handle fulfillment.

Who are the clinicians?

Physicians and nurse practitioners licensed in your state, engaged through our telehealth services partner. They are credentialed, background checked and covered by malpractice insurance before they review intakes. Most reviews happen without a live appointment. You can request a video visit at any time at no extra cost, and a video visit is required for the first prescription in Arkansas, the District of Columbia, Delaware, Kansas, Mississippi, New Mexico, Rhode Island and West Virginia.

What is your refund policy?

Cancel any time for any reason. Request a refund at any time for any reason. The one exception is a month whose medication has already been ordered for you, which cannot be refunded. If the clinician does not prescribe, nothing is ordered and your program payment is refunded in full. Medications are non-returnable once dispensed, as required by federal and state pharmacy law. See the Refund Policy.

Are there any fees besides the program price?

No. The price you see is the whole price. There is no separate consultation or physician fee, shipping is included, and for the semaglutide and tirzepatide programs the baseline labs at a national laboratory are included too.

How do I pay?

Visa, Mastercard, FSA and HSA cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay. American Express is not accepted. Billing is handled by our telehealth services partner, which is the name you will see on your card statement. Programs are paid out of pocket and are not billed to insurance.

Who is eligible?

You must be 18 or older and in the United States. GLP-1 programs are for adults 18 to 74 with a body mass index of 27 or above, or 25 or above with a related health condition. The microdosing program is for adults 18 to 74 with a BMI over 20. The clinician makes the final call on every intake.

Do I need lab work?

For the semaglutide and tirzepatide programs, yes. Baseline labs are ordered through a national laboratory at no cost to you, and in most cases you can start medication before the results are back, then the clinician reviews them at your first monthly check-in. For other programs the clinician will tell you if labs are recommended.

How long until my order arrives?

Once the clinician prescribes, the pharmacy prepares your medication and ships it to you. Ongoing plans ship every four weeks.

Can I cancel or pause any time?

Yes. Cancel at any time, for any reason, from your patient portal or by contacting support. Cancellation stops future charges. A month whose medication has already been ordered will still ship.

What if I have side effects or questions during treatment?

Patient support is available 24 hours a day, every day, and you can message your clinician or request a video visit through your patient portal. If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

Is my personal information private?

Your intake and health information are collected and stored by our telehealth services partner on a HIPAA-compliant platform, not on this website. See our Privacy Policy for details.

Which states do you serve?

All 50 US states and the District of Columbia. Every prescription requires review by a clinician licensed for your state, and your clinician may vary based on state availability.
