Peptide education

Peptides, explained simply

No jargon — just the basics, so you can make an informed decision with a physician.

What are peptides?

Your body is built from proteins — picture them as long strings of beads, where each bead is an amino acid. A peptide is simply a short string of those same beads.

Because they're small, peptides act like the body's messengers — little signals that tell cells what to do. Your body already makes thousands of them naturally, every day. In a medical setting, a physician may prescribe a specific peptide as part of a treatment plan.

Are they safe?

Safety depends on the person, the peptide, and the dose — which is exactly why every treatment here is prescription-only. An independent, licensed physician reviews your health history and decides whether a peptide is appropriate for you, and it's compounded by a licensed 503A pharmacy. Like any medication, peptides can have side effects and aren't right for everyone — that's a conversation to have with your physician.

What if I stop taking them?

Everyone responds differently, and it depends on the treatment and your daily habits. Some changes people work toward are supported by ongoing routines — sleep, nutrition, and movement — while other effects may gradually fade after stopping. There's no one-size-fits-all answer, so whether to continue, adjust, or stop is a decision to make with your physician.

How do I know if they're right for me?

The only way to know is a clinical evaluation. Start a free online visit — an independent physician reviews your goals and health history and tells you whether treatment is appropriate. If it isn't, they'll tell you that too.

How is it taken?

It depends on what's prescribed. Many peptide and GLP-1 treatments are given as a small subcutaneous self-injection — a tiny needle just under the skin, similar to insulin — usually on a weekly or daily schedule. Others may come in different forms. Your prescription ships with step-by-step instructions, and the care team can walk you through your first dose.

How are they made?

Prescribed treatments are prepared — or compounded — by licensed 503A compounding pharmacies in the U.S. that follow strict quality, sterility, and testing standards. Compounding lets a pharmacy prepare a medication to a physician’s specific prescription.

Peptides vs. GLP-1s — what's the difference?

GLP-1s (like semaglutide and tirzepatide) are actually a type of peptide. "Peptide" is the broad category — short chains of amino acids that signal the body — and GLP-1 receptor agonists are one specific, well-known group within it. Whether any of them is appropriate for you is a clinical decision made by the physician.

This section is general education, not medical advice. Whether any peptide is appropriate or safe for you is determined solely by an independent, licensed physician.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is Velacore Health?

Velacore Health is a telehealth platform. We coordinate access to independent, licensed physicians and 503A compounding pharmacies. We do not provide, guarantee, or facilitate any medical services, prescriptions, or treatments — all prescribing decisions are made solely by the independent physician.

Do I need a prescription?

Yes. Every treatment is prescription only. You complete an online visit, and an independent, licensed physician decides whether to prescribe based solely on clinical judgment. Prescriptions are issued only when clinically appropriate.

Does Velacore prescribe or provide medical care?

No. Velacore coordinates access and logistics. Independent, licensed physicians make all clinical decisions, and licensed 503A pharmacies handle fulfillment.

What is your refund policy?

Our promise is tied to the physician approval process. If the independent reviewing physician does not approve your program — in full or in part — your program payment is refunded in full within 15 days. The one-time $20 physician fee is non-refundable. Medications are non-returnable and non-refundable once dispensed by the pharmacy, as required by federal and state pharmacy law.

Are there any fees besides the medication?

Yes — a one-time $20 physician fee, paid when you complete your intake. It is collected on behalf of the independent medical group (not Velacore Health) for the physician's clinical review, and is non-refundable. Shipping is included at no additional charge. This fee is separate from the cost of any medication or program.

Will I talk to a real, licensed doctor?

Yes. An independent, licensed physician reviews your intake and makes every prescribing decision. Your day-to-day questions are handled by the Velacore care team, who escalate clinically significant concerns to the prescribing provider.

How long until my order arrives?

Once the physician approves your treatment, it's compounded by the pharmacy and shipped to you — typically arriving within a few business days. Timing can vary by pharmacy and your location.

Can I cancel or pause anytime?

Yes — there's no long-term contract. You can cancel or pause before your next fulfillment date by contacting the care team. Cancellation stops future charges; it doesn't refund a fulfillment that has already been dispensed.

Do you take insurance?

Programs are paid out of pocket and are not billed to insurance. Pricing is shown up front, with no surprise fees.

Is my personal information private?

Your information is handled on a privacy-conscious platform, and vendors that handle your health data operate under appropriate agreements. See our Privacy Policy for details.

What if I have side effects or questions during treatment?

Message the Velacore care team anytime — clinically significant concerns are escalated to the prescribing physician for review. If you're experiencing a medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

Do I need lab work or bloodwork?

It depends on your health history and the treatment. The independent physician will let you know if any labs are recommended as part of your evaluation.

Which states do you serve?

Velacore Health telehealth services are available in all 50 US states. Every prescription still requires review by an independent physician licensed for your state, and your prescribing provider may vary based on state availability.

Start an online visit. It's free to begin and helps the independent physician determine whether treatment is appropriate for you — with no obligation.
